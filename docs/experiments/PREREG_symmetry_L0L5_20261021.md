# Pre-registration: symmetry control, second attempt (L0 vs L5), written 2026-10-21 before any L5 flight

Replaces `PREREG_symmetry_L0L4_20261021.md` as the symmetry control. That control
failed by design, not by outcome: see its "Outcome" section. Everything below was
written after seeing the L4 flights and the RISE-fix flight, so those are disclosed
here and were used to design this one.

## What the first attempt taught

1. **The pair metric hid the defect.** L4 put the same hardware on faces 0 and 1 (CAD
   fitness 0.947 both directions) and left 2 and 3 bare (1.000). The mean index
   (0.716) looked like a broken symmetry; two pairs were still interchangeable and
   both observed closures were a +90 degree turn between exactly such a pair.
2. **The ICP fitness in `structure_symmetry.py` is one-way.** A face that is a subset of
   another scores 1.000 in one direction. The script now also prints the reverse
   direction and the pair minimum. Two faces are interchangeable only if each
   covers the other, so the pair score is the lower of the two.
3. **Closure time is not fixed.** On the stock scenario, DVL seed 0, the first closure was
   at 1034 s in flight 1 and 758 s in the RISE-fix flight. The three old L0 flights'
   closures at the start of the rise are therefore not a property of the manoeuvre
   and may have been provoked by the old RISE heading swing (untested).

## Arms

- **L0**: stock `bluerov2_turbine`. Pair minima all 1.000.
- **L5**: `bluerov2_turbine_sym_l5`, generated by `python3 eval/make_variants.py --levels L5`.
  Every face carries a different complement of the same two real hardware types used
  in L4 (risers and sacrificial anodes), plus a horizontal pipe run:
  - face 0 (bearing 180): three risers, at 4 m left, centre, 4 m right
  - face 1 (bearing 90): two columns of twelve anodes, 3 m either side of centre
  - face 2 (bearing 0): horizontal pipe runs at seven depths, +/- 8 m
  - face 3 (bearing 270): bare

**Manipulation check, fixed before any flight.** `eval/structure_symmetry.py --scn ...sym_l5.scn`,
pair minimum over both ICP directions at 1.0 m:

| pair | L0 | L4 | L5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | 1.000 | 0.947 | 0.751 |
| 0-2 | 1.000 | 0.590 | 0.776 |
| 0-3 | 1.000 | 0.591 | 0.694 |
| 1-2 | 1.000 | 0.584 | 0.787 |
| 1-3 | 1.000 | 0.587 | 0.685 |
| 2-3 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.688 |
| worst pair | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.787** |

Criterion met: no pair is above 0.8. Limits of this check, stated now: it is a geometric
proxy at 1.0 m on sampled surface, and says nothing about what ORB features the cameras
see. L5 is "every pair differs by a margin", not "every pair is unmistakable". A
null result at this margin does not rule out a stronger manipulation.

Everything else is identical across arms: **controller including the RISE change**,
SLAM configuration, bridge, `rviz:=false`, `dvl_error:=a50`, `dr_reference:=true`.
The old L0 flights 1 to 3 used the old RISE and are NOT used as comparators.

## Design

Paired by DVL unit. Seeds 0, 1, 2, then 29, 3, 26.

- **L0 seed 0** already exists under the new controller:
  `a50_risefix_seed0_20261021_205753` (flown before this pre-registration; disclosed).
- New L0 flights: seeds 1, 2 (then 29, 3, 26).
- New L5 flights: seeds 0, 1, 2 (then 29, 3, 26). Arm name `symL5`; L0 arm name `ctlL0`.

Run order within a stage: alternate L5 and L0 by seed (L5 s0, L0 s1, L5 s1, L0 s2, L5 s2) so
that drift in the machine or the day does not line up with one arm.

## Endpoint and test

**Primary, per mission:** the mission had at least one face-aliased closure, as classified by
`eval/closure_events.py` with the matching SLAM console log, at any time before the
vehicle starts RETURN_HOME. A mission with no closure counts as "no". (The first-closure
endpoint of the earlier pre-registration is replaced: closures can come in pairs and
the first one is not special.) Unscorable events are scored both ways; the conclusion must
hold under both or it is reported as unresolved.

**Test:** one-sided Fisher exact, L0 against L5, alpha 0.05, unit = mission.
With three per arm: 3/3 against 0/3 gives p = 0.05; 3/3 against 1/3 gives 0.20. Three per arm
can only ever just reach significance; that is why the second stage exists.
With six per arm: 6/6 against 2/6 gives 0.030, 6/6 against 3/6 gives 0.091.
**A non-significant result is reported as "not detected at this margin and power", never
as "no effect".**

**Secondary, descriptive:** the face pair of each aliased closure and its pair score;
closure time; fused-filter error after the closure; whether the vehicle ended within 1 m of home.
**Prediction on the mechanism (written now):** if symmetry is the cause, aliased closures in L5 should
be confined to the pairs that remain most alike (0-1 at 0.751, 1-2 at 0.787) and none should
turn between pairs scoring below 0.7. A closure between 1-3 or 2-3 or 0-3 would count against it.

## Void rules

Void if SLAM never initialises, the recorder fails, the vehicle collides, the mission does not
complete, the console log is missing, the DVL unit does not match the seed, or the orbit radius
(median over 150 to 900 s of truth) differs from the paired L0 flight by more than 1 m.
Voids are counted, reported, and repeated with the same seed and arm.

## Stop rules

1. **Gate B, after stage 1** (L5 seeds 0, 1, 2 and L0 seeds 0, 1, 2; five new flights):
   - L5 aliased in 2 or 3 of 3: no reduction detected at this margin. Stop. Report as such.
   - L5 aliased in 0 or 1 of 3 and L0 in 3 of 3: continue to stage 2 (seeds 29, 3, 26 in both arms).
   - L0 below 3 of 3: the baseline is not what we assumed; stop and reassess before any more flights.
2. No peeking inside a stage; each stage is completed or abandoned.
3. After stage 2, or after Gate B stops it, the paper is written with the result as it stands.
4. Flights before Gate B: at most 7 (5 plus 2 repeats for voids).
