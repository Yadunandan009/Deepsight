# Figures and tables: what exists, what each shows, how to regenerate

*Figures are numbered in order of first citation in the manuscript (renumbered 2026-10-24). 2026-10-22. Every figure is vector PDF plus a PNG for preview, in `eval/figures/`, in the journal style of `eval/pubstyle.py` (serif, boxed axes, inward ticks; 3.46 in single column, 7.16 in double). Check fonts and widths against the target journal's author guide. Liberation Serif stands in for Times.*

**One command regenerates everything that depends on flights:** `bash docs/paper/make_paper_assets.sh`. After each flight, add a row to `docs/experiments/flights_manifest.csv` and run it. It scores new flights only (cached by bag), rewrites `docs/paper/live_numbers.md`, and redraws the figures marked *live*.

## Captions (draft)

**Figure 1.** The testbed. The simulated world supplies cameras, a DVL carrying a per-unit scale and misalignment error, inertial, pressure and sonar data. ORB-SLAM3 (stock, stereo, in a container) feeds a bridge that converts frames, fits a Sim(2) transform, gates and resets. The primary filter fuses DVL, inertial, depth and the bridge's SLAM position; the controller steers on it. A second, SLAM-free filter exists only so the bridge's alignment can be fitted against something that does not depend on the alignment. Ground truth reaches the offline scorer and nothing else.

**Figure 2.** The simulated scene, from Stonefish's viewer (`eval/fig_scene.py`; screenshots in `docs/paper/stonefish_screens/`). (a) The jacket as modelled, with topside and crane, with the water rendering switched off in the viewer so that the whole structure is visible. (b), (c) Under water, with the viewer at about mid-depth: the structure fades into the water with distance (Jerlov 0.22). (d) The BlueROV2 Heavy at the surface, with the jacket's leg behind it. These are viewer screenshots, not images from the vehicle's stereo cameras, which were not recorded.

**Figure 3.** ORB-SLAM3 during a flight, two screenshots of its own viewer taken live (which flight and mission time were not recorded; no bag holds camera images) (`eval/fig_orbslam.py`). (a) The left camera image as ORB-SLAM3 receives it, after the image-enhancement node, facing one face of the jacket: green squares are features matched to map points, most on the edges of the X-braces and legs. Status bar: one map, 197 keyframes, 1767 map points, 199 matches. (b) The map seen from the side: black, all map points; red, the local map being tracked against; blue and green, the keyframes and the camera path, here the vertical line of a face sweep. The map is a sparse set of points on the brace edges, which is what the place recogniser matches between faces.

**Figure 4.** The structure from the CAD (`eval/fig_cad.py`), drawn east-north-up from the shaded CAD triangles. (a) The stock jacket from the south-east. (b) L5 from the south-east, so faces 0 (south) and 1 (east) face the reader. (c) L5 from the north-west, faces 2 (north) and 3 (west). Hardware on the faces turned away from the reader is drawn faded. (d) L5 seen square-on from outside each face, with only the half of the jacket nearer the viewer and that face's own hardware: three risers on face 0 (blue), two anode columns on face 1 (orange), seven horizontal pipe runs on face 2 (green); face 3 is bare. Water line blue, seabed brown, depth below the surface. The tower above the topside, the second structure and the seabed plane are not drawn. The jacket also carries a crane, boat landing and cable tubes that are not four-fold symmetric; the pair scores of Figure 5 are measured in the 1 to 22 m band the vehicle inspects.

**Figure 5.** How alike the faces are, measured from the CAD before any flight (`eval/fig_symmetry.py`). One grid per arm; each cell is the pair score of two faces, the lower of the two ICP fitness values (each face registered onto the other, and the reverse) at a 1.0 m inlier threshold; 1 means interchangeable. Values above the L5 criterion of 0.8 (the line on the colour bar) are in bold. L0, the stock jacket: every pair 1.00. L4: faces 2 and 3 stayed identical (1.00) and faces 0 and 1 nearly so (0.95). L5: worst pair 0.787.

**Figure 6.** The twelve loop closures classified in the earlier recordings. Wrong-face closures turn the estimate by about 90 degrees (once 180) about the structure's axis and keep its radius; benign closures correct by under 2 m. All five wrong-face closures end on the same side, but four start from the same face, so that is two independent cases.

**Figure 7.** True depth of the vehicle at each classified closure, by class (`eval/fig_where.py`, data from `eval/closure_where.py`). One dot per closure, spread sideways only so that dots do not overlap; bars are the medians (20.8 m wrong-face, 18.0 m benign). The shaded band is the 1 to 22 m scan; the dashed line at 20 m was chosen after looking at the data. Nine of twelve wrong-face closures fall at 20 m or more; ten of eleven benign closures fall between 14 and 19 m, on the starting face at the end of the mission. None of the L5 closures is at the bottom.

**Figure 8.** The structure rebuilt from the vehicle's own sonar in one earlier recording (2026-10-16, bridge version A, DVL unit 0, wrong-face closure at 573 s), coloured by each point's distance to the CAD (clipped at 1 m). Top (`recon_3d`): whole-run RMS under (a) the true poses, 7 cm, the sensor floor; (b) dead reckoning, 10 cm; (c) the primary filter fusing SLAM through bridge A, 1.8 m; (d) the trust-SLAM counterfactual, 4.5 m, where a second, rotated copy of the structure lies across the first. Bottom (`recon_explain`): (a) share of reconstructed points within a given distance of the CAD; (b) RMS in 10 s windows through the mission; after the closure 98 % of dead-reckoned points are within 25 cm, against 6 % fused and 1 % trusting SLAM. Whole-run values; Section 4.3.1 quotes the trust-SLAM RMS after the closure only (6.6 m here). Bridge A results are not used in the fusion comparison of Section 4.4.

**Figure 9.** ORB-SLAM3's own map in the same recording as Figure 8, placed by a similarity fitted to the true trajectory (no CAD used) and coloured by distance to the CAD (clipped at 1 m). (a) The sonar reconstruction under dead reckoning, for comparison: 99 % of points within 25 cm. (b) The last map before the wrong-face closure: 21 %. (c) The map after it: 7 %. The map includes features that are not on the bare jacket (seabed, hardware, outliers), so the share within 25 cm is a measure of agreement with the jacket, not of map error alone.

**Figure 10.** The cost of one wrong-face closure, same recording as Figures 8 and 9. (a) Bearing error about the structure's axis: dead reckoning (dashed) and the SLAM pose with its alignment held fixed from before the closure (solid); the closure at 573 s turns the estimate by +88 degrees. (b) Horizontal position error, 10 s RMS, log scale: the SLAM pose goes from tenths of a metre to 10 to 30 m and does not come back; dead reckoning stays near 0.1 m. Triangles: map re-initialisations. The solid curves are the counterfactual of a vehicle that trusts SLAM, not what the bridge did.

**Figure 11.** One wrong-face closure followed through a bridge-B flight (stage-1 stock flight L0 seed 0, the 180-degree closure; `eval/fig_story.py`). (a) Plan view: the true track (black), dead reckoning (dashed), the primary filter that fuses SLAM, and ORB-SLAM3's raw pose placed by a similarity fitted on the 150 s before the closure (residual 0.11 m), which jumps 33 m across the structure at 758 s. Red dots are the poses the bridge published after it re-locked, during the return home. (b) Horizontal error against the truth: the fused filter, dead reckoning, and each published SLAM pose. After the closure the bridge is silent (grey) for 219 s; its first poses after the re-lock are about 3 m wrong, the 5 m gate admits them, and the fused error rises above 4 m and ends 4.7 m wrong, while dead reckoning stays under 0.2 m.

**Figure 12.** Horizontal error against ground truth through each mission, with bridge version B. Solid: the primary filter (fuses SLAM; the vehicle steers on it). Dashed: the SLAM-free filter, same flight. Triangles on the axis: wrong-face closures; circles: benign ones. The dotted line is the bridge's 5 m gate. Dead reckoning stays within tenths of a metre throughout; the primary filter rises to just under the gate after a wrong-face closure and, in L5, after the map collapses at the start of the return home.

**Figure 13.** Is the fused error capped by the gate? Orange: the primary filter's error. Dashed: dead reckoning's. Dots: for each SLAM pose the bridge published, its distance from the dead-reckoned filter, the quantity the gate tests.

**Figure 14.** Where each mission ended, one bar per bridge-B flight and the earlier-bridge flight (`eval/fig_endstate.py`). (a) Final horizontal error of the primary (fused) filter; the dashed line is the bridge's 5 m gate. (b) True final distance of the vehicle from home, where the primary filter steered it; the dashed line is the 2 m radius at which the controller declares the mission complete. Orange: the flight had a wrong-face closure; grey: it did not.

**Figure 15.** Reconstruction RMS to the CAD, by flight and navigation source (log axis). The trust-SLAM bar is the RMS after the closure only and is a counterfactual.

**Figure 16.** The control. **(a)** One row per stage-1 flight (stock L0 above, L5 below; seeds 0, 1, 2); markers at the time of each closure: filled triangle wrong-face (the turn in degrees above it), open circle benign, cross unscorable. **(b)** Size of each correction against its turn about the axis for every closure in the manifest, by arm; grey dots are the earlier recordings; bands mark 15 degrees around 90 and 180; the dashed line is the 3 m benign threshold.

**Figure 17.** Distance of ORB-SLAM3's map points to the bare-jacket CAD, last map before the first closure. **(a)** Map placed by a similarity fitted to the true trajectory (no CAD used): the two arms look alike. **(b)** Map placed as well as possible against the CAD: the L5 map fits worse. The CAD omits L5's hardware, so points on hardware against a leg escape the count.

**Figure 18.** Three closure checks scored offline on the eleven cleanly classified closures of the earlier recordings (five wrong-face, six benign), with dead-reckoning drift injected into the true track, growing linearly with distance (`eval/gate_sweep.py`, 20 draws per level). (a) Share of wrong-face closures caught; (b) share of benign closures wrongly flagged. Size: the correction exceeds a tolerance plus three times the dead-reckoning uncertainty. Revisit: the dead-reckoned track says the vehicle has not been near the place. Chord: the correction equals the chord of a symmetry operation of the orbit (24.0 or 34.0 m for R = 17 m). Dark band: the drift of one lap. The size and revisit checks begin to miss at 7 m and catch none by 18 m; the chord check first raises a false alarm at the 24 m level sampled. The chord check was specified after the wrong-face chords had been seen (in-sample), the revisit check would not flag the return-time closures of the later flights (Section 4.6), and nothing here ran in the loop.
